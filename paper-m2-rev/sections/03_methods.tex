\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data, Cohort, and Chronological Split}
\label{sec:methods-cohort}

We used MIMIC-IV-ED~\cite{johnson2023mimicived}, the ED module of MIMIC-IV~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv},
through the MIMIC-IV-ED benchmark pipeline~\cite{xie2022mimic4ed}. The data come from one US academic medical center and are used
retrospectively as a research proxy; no result is a clinical validation. We analyzed the de-identified records under the PhysioNet credentialed data use agreement. \datagap{MIMIC-IV hospital-module version, to be confirmed by the author}

Of \RVMasterStays{} ED stays, \RVEligibleStays{} met the inclusion criteria (age of at least 18 years and a recorded triage acuity).
Calendar time is known only to about one year, from the anchor-year group of MIMIC-IV. Stays up to \RVCutYear{} form the training period (\RVTrainStays{} stays of \RVTrainPatients{} patients) and
later stays the test period (\RVTestStays{} stays of \RVTestPatients{} patients). Patients with stays on both sides of the cut remain in the
training period, and their \RVExcludedStays{} later stays (\RVExcludedPatients{} patients) were excluded from evaluation, so the test period has
no patient in common with the training period. Within the training period, patients were ordered by the year of their first stay and
divided into training, validation, calibration-selection, and final-calibration subsets of \RVDevTrainPatients{}, \RVDevValPatients{},
\RVDevCsPatients{}, and \RVDevFcPatients{} patients, the most recent patients in the two calibration subsets.

\subsection{Prediction Task: Landmarks and Outcomes}
\label{sec:methods-task}

The audited task is the end-of-stay task of an earlier, unpublished analysis of this cohort by the author: it predicts in-hospital death or ICU transfer within 12 hours of ED departure from the static profile and the last ten ED vital-sign rows of the stay, so in effect at departure. It enters only the input audit, the shortcut diagnostic, and a check of the conformal implementation.

A prediction is made at a landmark $L$ using only what has been recorded by $L$~\cite{vanhouwelingen2007landmarking}. The primary landmark is two
hours after ED arrival and the secondary landmark is arrival itself; because the triage table carries no timestamp, arrival is an operational anchor. A stay is eligible at $L$ if the patient is still in the ED
at $L$ and has had no ICU admission and no in-hospital death at or before $L$. The primary outcome is
$Y_L = 1$ if an ICU admission or an in-hospital death occurs in $(L, L + 24\,\mathrm{h}]$; outcomes are observed in hospital
only. A secondary outcome keeps the episode endpoint of the benchmark, restricted to eligible stays whose event follows $L$. At the 2\,h landmark the
cohort has \RVHtwoEligibleTotal{} eligible stays, with \RVHtwoPrimaryPositivesTotal{} primary-outcome and \RVHtwoSecondaryPositivesTotal{}
secondary-outcome positives (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}, Fig.~\ref{fig:landmark}); their relation to the endpoint of the benchmark is in the Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{Inputs and Their Timing}
\label{sec:methods-inputs}

Each stay is described by a static profile (age, sex, triage acuity, and the Elixhauser comorbidity count~\cite{quan2005coding}) and a sequence of
seven vital signs: the triage measurement, then every ED measurement charted between arrival and the landmark, truncated to the
\RVTEdHtwo{} most recent. The comorbidity count uses only the diagnoses of earlier admissions that ended before arrival, within \RVElixWindowDays{} days, so the codes of the index admission, which are finalized only after discharge~\cite{ramadan2025icdleakage}, are excluded. We audited every input against its source table and
timestamp (Table~\ref{tab:timing}). The recording practice differs between the periods (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}); we report this and do not correct it.

\subsection{Models and Tuning}
\label{sec:methods-models}

At the 2\,h landmark we compared five models. Hybrid GRU-D trained end to end is the primary model. It fuses a GRU-D encoder~\cite{che2018grud}, which decays each variable toward its training mean at learned rates that depend on the time since it was last observed, with a
static-feature branch, and trains both jointly. A frozen-encoder variant (an ablation) uses the encoder of a Temporal-Only GRU-D trained on
the same inputs and fits only the static branch and the head; the Temporal-Only GRU-D uses the vital-sign sequence alone. A timing-aware gradient-boosted
tree ensemble~\cite{chen2016xgboost} receives, for each vital sign, the mean, minimum, maximum, last value, and count, the static features, and
timing features (the number of ED rows, the time span and intervals between measurements, and the time since the last measurement). Logistic regression receives the same features. At the arrival
landmark we compared logistic regression with gradient-boosted trees on the triage vital signs and the static profile.

Every tuned model received four configurations and the same three tuning seeds, selected by validation AUROC, with the search spaces written to file before any tuning run (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:tuning}; training details in the Supplementary Methods).
Each selected configuration was refitted with five seeds $\{42, 123, 7, 456, 789\}$. The test period was never used for tuning, selection, or thresholds.

\subsection{Conformal Calibration}
\label{sec:methods-conformal}

Conformal methods were applied to the primary model at miscoverage $\alpha = 0.10$ and in a sweep over $\alpha \in \{0.20, 0.05, 0.01\}$. A method returns a
\emph{prediction set} $\widehat{C}(x) \subseteq \{0,1\}$, which covers a stay when it contains the observed class; a set with both labels, or with none,
is an abstention. \emph{Static split-conformal} calibration uses
the least-ambiguous (LAC) score~\cite{sadinle2019lac}: the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile $\hat{q}$ of the scores of the true
labels ($1-\hat{p}_k(x)$ for label $k$) on the final-calibration subset, fixed for the whole test period; our closed form reproduced the coverage of MAPIE~\cite{cordier2023mapie} to within \RVStaticVsMapie{}. \emph{Mondrian-LAC}~\cite{vovk2003mondrian} computes one quantile per observed class.
ECI~\cite{wu2025eci} extends adaptive conformal inference~\cite{gibbs2021aci} with an error-quantification term in the update of the radius after each label (equations and settings in the Supplementary Methods); we used the implementation of its authors and an event-driven re-implementation of the same update for delayed labels and per-class streams. ECI runs as one stream over the
final-calibration subset followed by the test period, and \emph{Mondrian-ECI} runs one
stream per observed class. \emph{Periodic recalibration} recomputes the static quantile every $R = 2{,}000$ arrivals from the most recent $W = 10{,}000$ labeled arrivals.

Labels were also delayed by $L_d \in \{60, 240, 1{,}000\}$ arrivals, and a controlled
drift test imposed a logit shift of $+0.5$ or $+1.0$, or a case-mix change, at the midpoint of the test stream; it is a stress test with synthetic drift and not a finding about the hospital.
For every set method we report the class-1 coverage and, from the cross-tabulation of set type against the true class, the positive predictive value (PPV) and sensitivity of the
singleton set $\{1\}$, the number of false alarms per 100 patients, and the abstention rate, and we compare them with a plain threshold on the precision--recall curve of the score. As a competitor that does not use conformal prediction, the intercept of the score was recalibrated on recent labeled arrivals, with the conformal quantile recomputed on shifted calibration scores (Supplementary Methods).

\subsection{Comparators and Diagnostic Analyses}
\label{sec:methods-diagnostics}

NEWS2~\cite{rcp2017news2} was computed from five of its seven parameters (level of consciousness and supplemental oxygen are not in the
extract), with a missing parameter contributing 0 points. The shortcut diagnostic asks how much of the discrimination of the end-of-stay task needs no physiological value. We fitted logistic
regression to quantities that describe how a stay was monitored and not how the patient was: ED duration, the number of
retained ED rows, and the number of observed values of each vital sign among them (the workflow set). To separate what monitoring adds from what is known at arrival, the same model was fitted to arrival information (the triage vital signs and their missingness, age, sex, triage acuity, and the comorbidity count), to arrival information with the workflow quantities, and to arrival information with ED duration alone; differences of AUROC use paired bootstrap resamples.

We characterized the recording practice by calendar year (Supplementary Methods). To test whether the copied row or the timing features drive the landmark results, logistic regression and the tree ensemble were refitted with the same stays, outcome, tuning budget, and seeds in three variants: with the copied first row removed, without the timing features, and with the vital-sign values and static profile only. The stored recurrent models were not refitted; each seed was scored on the test stays with the copied row removed, under a rule for a refit of Hybrid GRU-D (a change of \RVGrudRule{} or more in the seed-mean AUROC) fixed before the result was seen (Supplementary Methods).

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:methods-stats}

Discrimination is reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) over the five seeds, which measures training variability. Sampling uncertainty is reported
separately as a 95\% percentile interval from a patient-cluster bootstrap, in which patients are resampled with all their stays (\RVBootMain{} resamples for the
primary outcome, 1,000 for secondary analyses). Five contrasts of AUROC were specified before the results: C1 to C4 at the 2\,h landmark (Hybrid GRU-D minus the timing-aware tree
ensemble, minus logistic regression, minus Temporal-Only GRU-D, and minus the frozen-encoder variant) and C5 at the arrival landmark (trees minus logistic regression). The two-sided bootstrap values of the seed-averaged differences were adjusted with the Holm procedure, and a contrast is called significant only if its adjusted value is below 0.05 and all five per-seed 95\% intervals exclude zero. Calibration was summarized by the slope and intercept of a logistic regression of the outcome on the logit of the score~\cite{vancalster2019calibration}, and discrimination was also computed by sex and by age below and at or above 65 years.
Conformal coverage is judged against a validity threshold of 0.89, set before analysis slightly below the 0.90 target, and is summarized over seeds, because a
patient-cluster bootstrap is not valid for online methods; for the static methods, which apply a fixed threshold to independent patients, we also report the bootstrap 95\% interval of the seed-averaged coverage and the share of resamples that reach the threshold.

An analysis plan written before any landmark model was trained fixed the cohort, outcomes, inputs, models, tuning budget, contrasts, conformal methods, and the end-of-stay diagnostics. Other analyses were added after the first results, some after an external review, and are marked as added where they are reported (Supplementary Methods). No analysis selected a parameter on the test period.
