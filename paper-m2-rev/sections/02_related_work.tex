\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Leakage, meaning information in the model input that would not be available when the model is used, has been documented across machine-learning-based
science~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}. In MIMIC-IV, diagnostic codes that are finalized only after discharge inflate the performance of same-admission
models~\cite{ramadan2025icdleakage}. The presence and timing of measurements can themselves carry information about a patient~\cite{sisk2021informativepresence}, and the value of such a predictor depends on whether the mechanism that generates the measurements transports to another setting~\cite{groenwold2020informativemissingness}. Landmarking restricts both the inputs and the at-risk population to what holds at a chosen
time~\cite{vanhouwelingen2007landmarking}. The
MIMIC-IV-ED benchmark~\cite{xie2022mimic4ed} predicts at triage. A leakage-aware benchmark restricts the inputs to the first hour of presentation~\cite{solaiman2026earlydeterioration}, and CAREBench matches the length of the observed history between patients with and without the event~\cite{keoliya2025carebench}. These designs prevent a timing shortcut; they do not measure how large it is. In a recorded search (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:search}), none of \RVLitHitsOne{} records on ED prediction models with workflow
shortcuts or leakage, of the first 60 of \RVLitHitsSix{} records on informative presence, monitoring frequency, or label leakage in clinical prediction, or of \RVLitHitsSeven{} records on ED models with a stated prediction time quantified how much of the discrimination of an end-of-stay model comes from quantities that describe how a stay was monitored.

Split conformal prediction gives finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability~\cite{lei2018distributionfree,vovk2022algorithmic}; for classification we use the
least-ambiguous set-valued score~\cite{sadinle2019lac} as implemented in MAPIE~\cite{cordier2023mapie}. Clinical applications have calibrated sepsis mortality
risk~\cite{yang2024sepsisconformal}, sepsis diagnosis with external validation~\cite{dalal2025sepsisconformal}, and disposition at ED triage~\cite{abdulai2025idontknow}; they use static
calibration, evaluated on a random split, an external cohort, or later-admitted patients. Marginal coverage does not imply coverage within a minority class; Mondrian conformal prediction calibrates within
categories such as the observed class~\cite{vovk2003mondrian,ding2023classconditional}, and a recent preprint on multiple sclerosis imaging found that marginal sets
under-covered the minority class under distribution shift~\cite{millar2026classconditionalms}. For shift over time,
adaptive conformal inference updates the miscoverage level after each observation~\cite{gibbs2021aci}, and ECI adds an error-quantification term to the adaptive update~\cite{wu2025eci}. Among the screened records (\RVLitHitsTwo{} on conformal prediction
with ED, triage, deterioration or intensive care; \RVLitHitsThree{} on class-conditional conformal prediction in clinical data; \RVLitHitsFour{} on adaptive or online
conformal prediction under shift in clinical data; \RVLitHitsFive{} on delayed feedback), none reported the coverage of an ED deterioration model separately for the
patients who deteriorate together with the false-alarm burden of the sets, and none compared online methods with static and periodically recalibrated calibration on a
clinical stream. This study audits each input of an end-of-stay model against its source timestamp, measures what quantities that describe how the stay was monitored add to arrival information, and compares an adaptive method with static and periodic recalibration on a clinical stream with label delay. It does not propose a new prediction or conformal method.
